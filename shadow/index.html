Where these numbers come from

Two of these companies publish average revenue per user directly. Meta did, by region, every quarter, until it stopped in 2024. Reddit still does, United States against everywhere else — but only as a quarterly figure, so its row here is that quarterly number multiplied by four, stated plainly rather than left looking like a missing decade. For the other three we divide a revenue figure by a user count, each from the sources listed — Google's user count comes from a company blog post, not the filing its revenue does; X's is total US revenue rather than advertising alone; and Snapchat's, like Reddit's, is already a total-revenue figure by the company's own definition rather than advertising alone, with its user count from an investor letter rather than the filing its revenue comes from. The denominators are not even the same shape: some count monthly users, some count daily ones. Adding this together gives you an order of magnitude, not an audit.

Three companies are not in this table at all: Amazon's ad business, TikTok, and LinkedIn. None of the three has ever published a figure that pairs a user count with a matching revenue number, for any year, in any filing we could find. Guessing was the alternative to leaving them out, and we did not.

Most of the years in this table were never disclosed by anyone. Where a company published nothing for a year you were there, we draw a line between the years it did publish, and we start from nothing in the year the company began selling ads. The Evidenced column tells you, row by row, how many of your years rest on a number somebody actually filed — where it says four of twenty-three, believe the four, and treat the rest as our straight line through the dark. The total above is split the same way: what came from a filed year, and what we estimated to fill the gaps.

The dividend applies each company's own net margin to what you generated — what was left after every engineer, every data centre, and every line of overhead. Three of these five lost money in the last year they reported, so their share of the total is negative, not zero: a company with no surplus has nothing to divide, and less than nothing once its own losses are counted in.

Money you handed over for something you wanted is a purchase, not an extraction — but it is not cleanly separated out here. Where a row uses a company's total revenue rather than advertising alone — Meta, X, Snapchat and Reddit, all named above — a small slice of it is subscription money you handed over on purpose, such as Snapchat+ or Reddit Premium. Only Google/YouTube's row is advertising revenue specifically. We have not tried to strip the rest out; nobody publishes it in a form that would let us.

Every figure here was checked against a filing or other primary source on 10 September 2026; the source list is published with this page — sources.json and revenue-model.json.

Two · The shadow

Now the part they'd rather you didn't look at.

Every major platform is required by law to give you a copy of what it holds on you. Almost nobody opens the file. It is a ZIP full of JSON, and it is a portrait of your life written by a stranger who has been watching for a decade.

Drop your data export here
Instagram · Facebook · Google Takeout — the .zip, exactly as it downloaded

This works on anyone's export, not only your own. If the file in your hand belongs to somebody else, what appears below is a portrait of them — their hours, their people, what they were afraid of. We cannot stop that, and it would be dishonest to pretend this tool is what makes it possible; the archive is. Open your own.

How to get yours, checked 10 September 2026: Instagram, Facebook, Google Takeout (pick JSON where a format is offered). Wait times vary by platform and are not always published; Google's own page states a few minutes to a few days. Full detail and sourcing per platform, including the ones this page cannot yet read, is in export-buttons.json.

Three · The turn

None of this had to be the arrangement.

The shadow is yours — it would not exist if you did not. The value built on it is real, and it went somewhere. It is possible to build the same rooms on a different deed: where you hold the shadow, where the work is paid openly, and where what's left over is divided equally, one person, one share.

That's not a product. It's a constitution, and it hasn't been written yet.

We the Users is an open project, not affiliated with any company. The argument for it is already written and public. The thing itself — the constitution, the commonwealth it describes — has not been built yet. This page, and the tool above it, are evidence toward that argument: not the finished thing.
